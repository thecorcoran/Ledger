Planning Commission holds hearing on 2027-2032 capital facilities plan

Updated Sep 21, 2026 • Matter ID: File 26-0727

Official Title: 6.A Preliminary 2027-2032 Capital Facilities Plan - Public Hearing | Docket: Olympia • 2026-09-21 | Stage: Hearing

Share · Ask a question — • Email • Ask a question

Why This Matters Now

The Olympia Planning Commission is currently holding an advisory review. This is the earliest and most direct window for public input to reshape draft code before formal recommendations are submitted to the Olympia City Council. This decision directly affects private real property owners across Thurston County whose annual tax statements would include the adjusted rates. The proposed ordinance (a local law passed by the council or county) establishes an updated multi-year rate assessment (a fee charged on property tax statements for a dedicated service) schedule for the conservation district under RCW 89.08.400. The Board of County Commissioners holds full local statutory discretion to approve, reduce, or reject the assessment schedule requested by the conservation district supervisors.

Who This Affects

  • Working agricultural parcel owner: Subject to proposed per-acre and per-parcel system of rates and charges under RCW 89.08.400 (Hearing Notice).
  • Rural residential homeowner: Subject to updated annual parcel rate assessments billed directly on county property tax statements.
  • Exempt parcels (forestland or tribal trust): The published preliminary hearing notice omits the complete parcel exemption list and tier schedules. The documents do not say.

What's Good Here

  • Voluntary Local Stewardship: Dedicated conservation district funding supports voluntary landowner assistance, wildfire protection, and farm planning without regulatory enforcement actions (RCW 89.08.010).
  • Non-General Fund Support: Establishes dedicated program revenue without diverting county general property taxes from sheriff or road services.

Our Reading

  • Cost and who pays: Leaves open the exact per-parcel dollar increase, acreage rate tiers, and total revenue levied on private landowners because the preliminary hearing notice omits the rate schedule (Hearing Notice Table 1). Our reading: Publishing a hearing notice before posting the actual fee schedule rests on the assumption that rate structures are technical details for later review, leaving property owners unable to calculate their tax bill before public testimony closes.
  • Consent and process: Narrows meaningful public participation by cutting off written testimony two hours before the hearing while the underlying assessment table remains unposted in the preliminary agenda (Notice Text). Our reading: This sequence assumes formal statutory notice satisfies public consent even when the specific rates under consideration are unavailable to residents preparing comments.
  • Subsidiarity: Keeps final approval authority with the locally elected Board of County Commissioners under RCW 89.08.400 to approve, modify, or reject the assessment requested by the conservation district (RCW 89.08.400). Our reading: Placing final rate authority with county commissioners preserves local political accountability, though taxpayers bear the cost if commissioners rubber-stamp an incomplete schedule.

What It's Trying to Do, and How Supporters Would Answer

The Goal: The proposed ordinance seeks to provide stable, multi-year local funding for the Thurston Conservation District so it can assist farmers and rural landowners with voluntary soil conservation, wildfire resiliency, and habitat stewardship (RCW 89.08.400). Supporting non-regulatory, voluntary stewardship is a constructive goal.

How Supporters Would Answer Our Concerns: Supporters note that conservation districts have no taxing authority of their own and rely on county-approved assessments to match state and federal grants; they argue the assessment is a modest per-parcel fee that keeps conservation voluntary rather than regulatory. If no action is taken, the district loses core operating funds and must cut technical assistance to local farms.

Goal vs. Means: Our concern is with the means and process, not the goal: funding voluntary conservation is worthwhile, but asking the public to testify before the exact per-parcel rate table is published in the agenda packet undermines informed taxpayer consent.

What Would Make It Better

  • Possible Ask (Clear Public Notice): Provide plain-language mailed summaries to affected neighbors before final votes.
  • Possible Ask (Performance Review Sunset): Include a three-year review date requiring staff to evaluate policy outcomes and public feedback.

What to Do

Who Decides Next: The Olympia Planning Commission is an advisory citizen body that conducts public reviews and makes recommendations. It does not pass ordinances. The Olympia City Council decides next whether to adopt the code into law.

Meeting & Next Step: The body reviewed this draft at its meeting on 2026-09-21. Next step not yet posted.

How to Comment: Submit written testimony online or by email to the staff liaison or clerk prior to the meeting cutoff (Check meeting agenda for registration cutoff), or sign up to testify at scheduled public hearings.
