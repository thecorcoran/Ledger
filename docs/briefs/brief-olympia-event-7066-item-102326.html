Planning Commission holds hearing on critical area land rules

Official Title: 6.A Critical Area ordinance (a local law passed by the council or county) Update - Public Hearing | Docket: Olympia • 2026-10-05 | Stage: Hearing

For Staff

  • Statutory Constraint Link (Washington Growth Management Act): The matter engages mandate constraints under official state/federal rules (6.A Critical Area Ordinance Update - Public Hearing Body: Planning Commission Meeting Details: Register to...) which are not cross-referenced in the local staff summary.

Why This Matters Now

The Olympia Planning Commission is currently holding an advisory review. This is the earliest and most direct window for public input to reshape draft code before formal recommendations are submitted to the Olympia City Council. This matter directly affects thousands of rural and suburban landowners whose properties contain or border wetlands, streams, or seasonal water drainage across unincorporated Thurston County. The proposed code revises standard wetland buffer categories, updates scientific rating manuals, and clarifies buffer-averaging and mitigation requirements. Under the Washington Growth Management Act (RCW 36.70A.172), the county is required by state law to include Best Available Science when protecting critical areas. However, county commissioners retain local policy choice over buffer reduction allowances, reasonable-use exceptions (TCC 24.45), and agricultural exemptions.

What Changes

What's Good Here

  • Clear Regulatory Alignment: Brings county code into alignment with state wetland rating manuals, reducing confusion between state and county biologists during permit reviews (TCC 24.30.025).
  • Preserves Reasonable Use: Retains standard administrative reasonable-use relief provisions so parcels heavily encumbered by wetlands still have a viable legal path to build a modest home (TCC 24.45).
  • Protects Drinking Water Aquifers: Safeguards natural wetland sponge functions and groundwater recharge areas that protect rural drinking water wells from contamination and reduce downstream flooding for neighboring landowners.

Our Reading

  • Ownership: Narrows the usable yard, garden, and building space on private parcels by expanding standard wetland buffer widths without providing property tax adjustments or direct compensation (Table 24.30-1). Our reading: This approach rests on the premise that private land bordering wetlands should function as uncompensated public ecological infrastructure, leaving individual rural and suburban homeowners to bear the loss of usable land.
  • Subsidiarity: Shifts local land-use rulemaking toward state agency administrative tables by adopting Department of Ecology buffer guidance without documenting local Thurston soil or hydrology exceptions (TCC 24.30.045). Our reading: Treating state agency guidance ranges as fixed local baselines assumes uniform statewide tables fit every county drainage basin, reducing the role of elected commissioners in tailoring rules to local rural conditions.
  • Small and local vs. large and distant: Concentrates compliance costs on ordinary families and small builders by requiring professional wetland delineations and mitigation reports as a condition of permit completeness (TCC 24.30.070). Our reading: Requiring specialized biological studies before basic permit review assumes every applicant has institutional capital, which large subdivision developers can spread across dozens of lots while individual homeowners face a high upfront barrier.

What It's Trying to Do, and How Supporters Would Answer

The Goal: The draft update aims to protect Thurston County's wetlands so they continue absorbing seasonal floodwaters, filtering pollutants before they reach rural drinking-water wells, and sustaining summer streamflows (TCC 24.30.010). That goal is sound—preventing downstream flooding and aquifer contamination protects everyone's property and health.

How Supporters Would Answer Our Concerns: Supporters and county staff argue that uniform, science-based buffers derived from Department of Ecology rating manuals are the only reliable way to prevent cumulative habitat loss across rapidly growing basins, and that case-by-case exceptions allow piecemeal degradation. If the county takes no action, Thurston County falls out of compliance with the Growth Management Act (RCW 36.70A.172), exposing the county to Growth Management Hearings Board invalidation orders and the loss of state infrastructure grants.

Goal vs. Means: Our concern is with the means, not the goal: protecting wetlands and aquifers is vital, but the county can achieve that goal through property-tax relief for conserved buffer land, county-funded delineation assistance, and reasonable-use protections rather than shifting the full financial and land-use burden onto individual homeowners.

What Would Make It Better

  • Possible Ask (Separate state-mandated requirements from locally chosen additions): Request that staff clearly separate requirements strictly mandated by state statute or administrative rules from optional local policy additions, and evaluate whether the local extras are necessary.
  • Possible Ask (Protect existing legal uses, homes, yards, and gardens): Add an explicit safe-harbor provision confirming that existing legally established residential homes, maintained yards, gardens, and customary outbuildings are exempt from retroactive buffer expansions or new ongoing maintenance restrictions.
  • Possible Ask (Provide an accessible reasonable-use exception path for small or constrained lots): Include an administrative, low-cost reasonable-use exception process so that owners of small, historic, or legally platted lots who cannot meet full expanded buffers can still build a modest single-family home without costly quasi-judicial hearing fees.

What to Do

Who Decides Next: The Olympia Planning Commission is an advisory citizen body that conducts public reviews and makes recommendations. It does not pass ordinances. The Olympia City Council decides next whether to adopt the code into law.

Meeting & Next Step: The body reviewed this draft at its meeting on 2026-10-05. Next step not yet posted.

How to Comment: Submit written testimony online or by email to the staff liaison or clerk prior to the meeting cutoff (Check meeting agenda for registration cutoff), or sign up to testify at scheduled public hearings.
