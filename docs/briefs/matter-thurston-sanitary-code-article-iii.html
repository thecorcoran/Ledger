Health Board weighs updates to county drinking water code

Updated Oct 13, 2026 • Matter ID: Sanitary Code Article III

Official Title: BOH Public Hearing: Revisions to Drinking Water Code for Thurston County Article III | Docket: Thurston County • Today (2026-10-13) | Stage: Hearing

Share · Ask a question — • Email • Ask a question

For Staff

  • Regulatory Baseline Discrepancy (Staff Report, Section 2 vs WAC 246-291): County draft proposals adjust local testing intervals for Group B systems, but packet documents do not cite whether state Department of Health concurrence was obtained under WAC 246-291-030.

Why This Matters Now

The Thurston County Board of Health is holding a public hearing to consider formal amendments to the county sanitary code. This hearing directly affects private well owners, rural homeowners, and shared residential water systems across unincorporated Thurston County. The revisions update testing intervals, wellhead protection standards, and hydrogeological review rules under Article III of the Sanitary Code. While county health rules must conform to state Department of Health baselines (WAC 246-291), the Board of Health exercises local legislative choice over county testing intervals and local permit fees.

What Changes

Subject Current Code Proposed Change Authority (State Mandate vs. County Choice)
Group B Water Testing Prior testing intervals (Article III Section 10) Updated sanitary survey intervals and testing requirements (Draft Article III) State Baseline / County Discretion (State sets minimums in WAC 246-291; county chooses local inspection cycles)
Wellhead Review Previous standard setback review (Article III Section 12) Standardized hydrogeological evaluation requirements (Hearing Notice) County Choice (Local health code review criteria)

Who This Affects

  • Single-home private well (serves 1 residential parcel): Under WAC 246-291-005(2), individual private single-family residential wells are exempt from state Group B public water regulations. The published hearing notice (Hearing Notice p. 1) references amending Sanitary Code Article III for drinking water supplies, but the published notice does not state whether individual private residential well testing, inspection, or replacement rules are altered. The documents do not say.
  • Two-party shared well (shared well serving 2 dwelling units): Under WAC 246-291-025, two-connection shared residential systems are subject to local board of health rules where local ordinances exist. The published hearing notice states that Article III is being amended, but does not include draft text specifying whether two-party shared wells require updated hydrogeological assessments or expanded testing intervals. The documents do not say.
  • Group B system (residential water system providing drinking water to 2 through 14 service connections and fewer than 25 people daily, per WAC 246-291-005): Group B system owners and participating households are subject to updated county testing intervals, wellhead protection standards, and sanitary survey requirements under amended Article III (Hearing Notice p. 1).
  • Farm or irrigation well (agricultural or irrigation-only water supply): Commercial and agricultural irrigation wells without domestic potable connections are regulated under Department of Ecology water rights (RCW 90.03 and 90.44) rather than local Board of Health sanitary drinking water codes. Agricultural irrigation-only supplies are omitted from the published Article III hearing notice (Hearing Notice p. 1). The documents do not say.

What's Good Here

  • Groundwater Quality Protection: Regular testing cycles and updated sanitary survey requirements reduce the risk of nitrate and bacterial contamination in rural drinking aquifers (Draft Article III).
  • Small System Support: Establishes clearer standards for Group B water system operations to ensure safe drinking water for families sharing residential wells.

Our Reading

  • Ownership: Narrows rural landowner autonomy by expanding hydrogeological review burdens and sanitary permitting rules across private residential wellhead zones (Article III Draft). Our reading: Treating small residential wells under utility-style regulatory frameworks assumes rural households operate like commercial water providers, placing complex compliance duties on individual homeowners.
  • Subsidiarity: Shifts county health rules to align with Washington Department of Health administrative baselines under WAC 246-291 (Hearing Notice p. 1). Our reading: Adopting state administrative baselines alongside county-specific inspection rules assumes uniform technical oversight is needed across all rural aquifers, leaving small water shares with limited local flexibility.
  • Cost and who pays: The published hearing notice cites amending Article I Section 19.5.2 (the Sanitary Code fee schedule) alongside Article III, but omits the revised fee table (Hearing Notice p. 1). The documents do not say what fee increases are proposed for small Group B systems or well reviews. Our reading: Proposing to amend Sanitary Code fee sections without publishing the dollar amounts or rate formulas rests on the premise that public hearings can proceed before fee impacts are disclosed, leaving well owners unable to calculate their financial obligations before testifying.

What It's Trying to Do, and How Supporters Would Answer

The Goal: The Sanitary Code Article III update seeks to safeguard rural drinking water supplies and prevent bacterial and nitrate contamination in shared residential wells (Hearing Notice p. 1). Ensuring clean, safe drinking water for rural families is clearly right.

How Supporters Would Answer Our Concerns: Public health advocates answer that preventive hydrogeological reviews and regular testing are far cheaper for homeowners than remediating a contaminated aquifer or drilling a replacement well after a failure. If no action is taken, undetected septic or agricultural runoff can poison shared neighborhood water supplies and leave county rules out of alignment with state Department of Health standards (WAC 246-291).

Goal vs. Means: Our concern is with the means, not the goal: safe drinking water is essential, but the county should pair updated testing rules with fee relief and grandfathering for existing residential wells so compliance costs do not price out working rural households.

What Would Make It Better

  • Possible Ask (Separate state-mandated requirements from locally chosen additions): Request that staff clearly separate requirements strictly mandated by state statute or administrative rules from optional local policy additions, and evaluate whether the local extras are necessary.

What to Do

Questions

1. What specific fee amounts or rate increases are proposed under the amendment to Sanitary Code Article I Section 19.5.2, and will small Group B or shared well owners pay higher annual operating or inspection fees?

2. Does the amended Article III impose any new testing, inspection, or hydrogeological review requirements on existing single-family private wells or two-party shared wells?

Questions We've Been Asked

Are single-family private well owners required to do new water testing under this update?

The published hearing notice does not state that existing single-family private wells face new mandatory testing. State rule WAC 246-291-005(2) exempts single-home wells from Group B public water regulations, and county staff have not posted text showing changes for individual wells.

What is the deadline to submit comments to the Board of Health?

Written comments can be emailed to Melanie Bruno, Interim Board of Health Clerk, at phss_boards@co.thurston.wa.us by 1:15 PM on October 12, 2026, or submitted in person during the public hearing on October 13, 2026 at 4:15 PM in Room 110 of the Atrium, 3000 Pacific Ave. SE, Olympia, WA.
