Hearing Examiner holds hearing on Springwood Garden subdivision

Updated Sep 28, 2026 • Matter ID: Case 25-0980

Official Title: 2.B PUBLIC HEARING - Case: 25-0980, Springwood Garden plat | Docket: Olympia • 2026-09-28 | Stage: Hearing

Why This Matters Now

The Olympia Hearing Examiner is conducting a quasi-judicial land use hearing where public testimony is placed into the formal evidentiary record. The Hearing Examiner is conducting quasi-judicial permit review for the Springwood Garden subdivision proposal (Case 25-0980). The proposal creates 37 single-family residential homeownership lots on 7.2 acres with dedicated wetland open space tracts. Permit approval is governed by local municipal zoning codes and state subdivision statutes (RCW 58.17).

Who This Affects

  • Adjacent neighborhood homeowner: Affected by construction vehicle traffic, utility trenching, and permanent perimeter stormwater and wetland tract buffers (Application Narrative p. 2).
  • Prospective residential homebuyer: Creates 37 single-family residential homeownership lots on 7.2 acres within the urban growth area.
  • Project applicant / builder: Required to construct city-standard access streets, extend municipal water and sewer utilities, and record dedicated open space easements.

What's Good Here

  • Expands Homeownership Supply: Creates 37 new single-family fee-simple homeownership lots within the urban growth boundary where services already exist (Application Narrative p. 2).
  • Permanent Open Space: Dedicates sensitive wetland areas and perimeter buffers to shared open space tracts (Preliminary Plat Sheet C-2).

Our Reading

  • Ownership: Strengthens individual homeownership opportunities by creating 37 fee-simple residential lots on 7.2 acres within the urban growth boundary (Application Narrative p. 3). Our reading: Approving fee-simple infill subdivisions rests on the premise that expanding resident-owned housing supply inside existing service boundaries benefits working families seeking permanent roots.
  • Places and history: Shifts 7.2 acres of undeveloped neighborhood open space into residential lots while dedicating sensitive wetland buffers and perimeter tracts to permanent conservation (Site Plan Sheet C-2). Our reading: This layout assumes density inside city utility zones can coexist with ecological stewardship when wetland corridors are set aside in dedicated tracts, though adjacent neighbors bear the transition to higher traffic and density.

What It's Trying to Do, and How Supporters Would Answer

The Goal: The preliminary plat application seeks to build 37 single-family homes on a 7.2-acre infill parcel within the City of Olympia while setting aside wetland buffers in permanent conservation tracts (Application Narrative p. 2). Expanding family homeownership inside existing city utility boundaries is a constructive goal.

How Supporters Would Answer Our Concerns: Supporters and city planners emphasize that placing new homes inside the urban growth area prevents rural sprawl, utilizes existing city water and sewer mains, and meets all city stormwater and wetland setback codes. If no action is taken or code-compliant plats are blocked, housing prices rise further and the city faces legal liability under the Land Use Petition Act (RCW 36.70C).

Goal vs. Means: The concern for neighbors is about the means of site integration—ensuring stormwater facilities and perimeter buffers perform as designed so new density does not degrade adjacent yards or wetlands.
