How to Take Part

You don't need to be an expert to speak up about a local decision. This page covers four things: how to comment on something already scheduled, how to ask for something to be added to an agenda, how to write to staff and to officials, and how to keep track of the dates.

1. Commenting on an item that is already scheduled

Every brief on this site lists who decides, the meeting date, and the comment deadline when the documents give one. Most bodies take comment in one or more of these ways:

  • In writing, by email or an online form, usually due before the meeting. Some cutoffs are as short as two hours before it starts.
  • In person at the meeting.
  • By video call, often with a sign-up that closes before the meeting.

Written comments sent to the clerk or through the official portal go into the record the decision-makers receive. An email sent only to one official may not. When it matters, send it both ways.

Deadlines and rules change. Each brief links to the official agenda, and that is the final word.

2. Asking for something to be added to an agenda

Commenting on a scheduled item is different from asking a body to take something up. Agenda requests usually go to a chair, a mayor, a council member, a commissioner, or a clerk, and often need to be made weeks ahead. The rules differ by body.

Below is a short guide for each body we follow. Each entry shows when it meets, how it takes comment, how to ask for an agenda item, who to contact, and the date we last checked.

City of Olympia City Council

Municipal Legislative Body
When it meets: Meets Tuesdays at 5:30 PM or 7:00 PM, Council Chambers, Olympia City Hall, 601 4th Ave E, Olympia, WA. Study sessions on 1st/3rd Tuesdays; business meetings on 2nd/4th Tuesdays.
How it takes comment: Public comment period held during Tuesday meetings. Written comments submitted to citycouncil@olympiawa.gov or via portal by 4:00 PM on meeting day. Live Zoom/in-person testimony requires sign-up before 4:00 PM.

3. Writing to staff and writing to officials

These are two different kinds of email, and mixing them up is the most common reason a message doesn't land.

Staff answer questions. They wrote or manage the documents, and they can tell you what a section means, where something is, or how a process works. They don't decide the matter, and it isn't fair to ask them to take a side.

Officials make decisions. Council members, commissioners, and board members are the people to tell what you think and what you'd like them to do.

Writing to staff: asking a question

  • Look in the packet first. If you can't find your answer, say so: "I looked in the packet and couldn't find…"
  • Put the item and date in the subject line.
  • In the first two sentences, say who you are (a resident, and where), which item, and what you're asking.
  • Ask one specific question, ideally one that can be answered in a few lines or with a section number.
  • Keep it to about 100 to 150 words.
  • Write early, so an answer can arrive before the hearing.
Example starting draft for staff:
Subject: Question about [item] ([meeting date])

Hello [name],

I'm a resident of [area] reading the packet for [item] on [date]. I couldn't find where it says [what you're looking for]. Could you point me to the section?

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Your phone or address, optional]

Writing to officials: telling them what you think

  • Put the item, date, and your position in the subject line: "[Item]: support," "[Item]: oppose," or "[Item]: from a resident of [area]."
  • State your conclusion in the first sentence: what you're asking them to do (vote yes, vote no, amend, hold a hearing, take more time).
  • Give two or three reasons, grounded in your experience or in the packet. Numbers and specific sections help; general accusations don't.
  • If you have an alternative, say what it is: "Instead of X, please consider Y."
  • Thank them for their service, even if you disagree with them.
  • Keep it to about 200 to 300 words.
Example starting draft for officials:
Subject: [Item]: from a [city or area] resident

Hello [name],

I'm writing about [item] on the [date] agenda.

I'm concerned about [specific provision] because [one clear reason].

I'd ask that you [specific request, such as: amend section X to…, ask staff for…, or vote no on…].

Thank you for your service to our community.

[Your name]
[Your address or neighborhood — officials pay closest attention to their own constituents]
